PDF to Word OCR - User Manual
=============================

This app turns PDFs, scans and photos of documents (even handwriting) into
Word documents you can edit.

Everything happens on your computer. Your files are never uploaded, and no
internet is needed.


WHAT YOU NEED
-------------
- A Windows 10 or Windows 11 computer.
- About 3.5 GB of free disk space while setting up (the download is 1.1 GB
  and the unpacked app is 2.2 GB). You can delete the download afterwards.

There is nothing else to install.


FIRST TIME: SET IT UP (about 2 minutes)
---------------------------------------
1. Download PDFtoWordOCR-portable.zip.
   If Google Drive says it can't scan the file for viruses, click
   "Download anyway".
2. Right-click the ZIP, choose "Extract All...", then click "Extract".
3. Open the new PDFtoWordOCR folder and double-click PDFtoWordOCR.exe.
4. If a blue box says "Windows protected your PC", click "More info", then
   "Run anyway". It appears because the app doesn't come from the
   Microsoft Store.

Desktop icon: right-click PDFtoWordOCR.exe, then choose
"Send to" > "Desktop (create shortcut)". On Windows 11, click
"Show more options" first. Keep the .exe inside its folder, because it
needs the files next to it.


CONVERT A DOCUMENT
------------------
1. Click "Choose file..." and pick your PDF or picture (JPG, PNG and so
   on). You can also drag the file onto the window.
2. Choose the Mode (see "Which mode should I pick?" below).
3. Click the blue "Extract text" button. Wait until the line under the
   progress bar says "Finished".
4. Read the text in the big box and fix any mistakes. You can type in it,
   just like in Notepad.
5. Click "Save Word document". By default the Word file is saved next to
   your original file, with "_ocr" added to its name. To save it somewhere
   else, click "Browse..." next to "Save Word file as" first.


WHICH MODE SHOULD I PICK?
-------------------------
  A normal PDF made on a computer          ->  Auto
  (you can select its text)

  A scan or photo of a typed or            ->  Auto or Printed Text
  printed page, or a receipt

  Handwriting                              ->  Handwriting

Auto never switches to Handwriting by itself. If a handwritten page comes
out as gibberish, choose "Handwriting" yourself.

Handwriting mode is slower, about half a minute per page, and the first
page takes a little longer. It reads English handwriting.


TIPS FOR GOOD RESULTS
---------------------
- Use clear, straight, well-lit scans or photos.
- Receipts: lay the receipt flat, fill the photo with it, and avoid
  shadows. Crumpled or folded receipts still read poorly.
- Always double-check names, numbers and dates.
- Keep "Join lines into paragraphs" ticked for normal text. Untick it for
  poems, addresses or lists, to keep the original line breaks.
- Lines like "===== PAGE 2 ... =====" show where a new page starts. They
  become page breaks in Word. You can delete them if you don't want page
  breaks.
- "Language" and "DPI" can stay as they are. A higher DPI reads small
  print better but is slower.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
The text is gibberish
  The page is probably handwritten. Choose "Handwriting" mode and click
  "Extract text" again.

A "Low OCR confidence" message
  Same as above. If it isn't handwriting, try a clearer scan.

"... lines were too unclear to read and left out"
  Handwriting mode skipped lines it couldn't read, rather than guess.
  Type those lines in from the original.

It seems stuck
  Wait: handwriting takes a while. The progress bar and the status line
  show what it's doing. "Cancel" stops it.

"Could not write ... open in Word"
  Close that document in Word, then click "Save Word document" again.

"not a valid PDF or image file"
  The file is damaged, or it isn't a PDF or picture. Check that it opens
  in another program.

"Windows protected your PC"
  Click "More info", then "Run anyway".


PRIVACY
-------
Your documents never leave your computer, and the app works without
internet.


REMOVING THE APP
----------------
Delete the PDFtoWordOCR folder, and the desktop shortcut if you made one.
